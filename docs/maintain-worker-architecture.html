Maintain Worker

How the workforce exchange is built

Maintain Worker is Australia's business-to-business workforce exchange for construction. One company lists spare crew, another states a shortfall, and Maintain introduces the two. This document describes the system that runs it: its architecture, how a company account is approved, how the exchange actually moves, and why it is put together this way.

01

What the exchange is

Construction companies swing between two problems within the same month. In April a crew finishes early and sits idle. In May the same business wins work it cannot staff. Maintain Worker is the exchange where those two states meet, directly, between businesses.

The product has one audience with two postures. A company that has capacity available lists the crew it can spare. A company with a shortfall posts what it needs: trade, level, headcount, dates, hours, region. There is no buyer account and no supplier account, and no mode to switch between them. The same login does both, because the same business does both.

Every introduction in this version is made by a person at Maintain. The software finds and ranks the candidates, enforces the compliance rules and holds the commercial terms still, but a Maintain admin decides which proposal to put in front of which company. That is a deliberate choice about trust rather than a gap in the build.

The rule everything else follows

The unit of the marketplace is the company. A worker is never listed, never browsed, never searched and never named to the other side before a deal is commercially confirmed. There is no cross-company worker directory anywhere in the product. The hiring business approves a shape: a trade, a level, a headcount and a window. The supplying business chooses which of its people go.

This is what makes the compliance work meaningful. Verifying one ABN, one public liability policy and one workers compensation policy per business is tractable. Verifying them per person per job is not, which is the cost every head contractor currently pays.

System architecture

One deployable and one database. A single Next.js 16 App Router application on Vercel serves the public site, the company workspace and the Maintain admin console. Everything durable lives in one Supabase Postgres project.

What each piece is responsible for

Runtime responsibilities
ComponentOwnsNotes
Next.js 16 App Router Every surface: marketing site, company workspace under /app, admin console under /admin React 19, Tailwind v4, TypeScript strict. MDX pages are routable.
src/proxy.ts The edge gate. This Next.js version names middleware proxy.ts. Wraps clerkMiddleware. Redirects signed-out requests for /app* and /admin*, stamps X‑Robots‑Tag: noindex, and makes no role decision at all.
Clerk Credentials, sessions, invitations, second factors, and the Maintain staff flag in backend publicMetadata. The only authoritative identity provider. There is no Clerk webhook, so a role change lands on the user's next request.

Supabase Storage Two private buckets: company-documents and worker-qualifications. 10 MiB per file, PDF or JPEG or PNG only. Every read is a server-minted signed URL valid for 300 seconds.

Vercel Cron One schedule, 0 14 * * * UTC, which is midnight in Brisbane. Calls /api/cron/daily with a bearer secret. A deployment with no secret configured rejects every call.

Three database clients, two of which matter

The application opens a Supabase client in one of two ways. The tenant client carries the signed-in user's Clerk token, so Postgres evaluates row level security against that person's company. The service-role client bypasses row level security entirely and is only reached after an authorisation guard has already passed; it is how Maintain staff screens read across companies, and how every privileged state change is executed. A third, browser-side client survives in the tree but nothing reachable imports it.

03

Trust boundaries

Authorisation is checked at five separate depths, and the checks are deliberately redundant. The edge gate is optimistic and cheap. The real decisions are made next to the data.

The guard ladder

Four server-side guards sit between a request and the data. Each one is a superset of the last, and the message a blocked user sees names the actual reason.

Server-side authorisation guards
GuardPasses whenOtherwise
requireCompanyAdmin Signed in and bound to a company by an accepted membership. No session goes to /signin. No membership goes to /onboarding. An unaccepted membership goes to /accept-invitation.
requireWritableCompany Company status is Pending or Active. Throws with distinct copy for suspended and for closed.
requireActiveCompany Company status is Active only. Throws. A Pending company is told verification is still in progress.
requireMaintainAdmin Clerk backend publicMetadata marks the account as Maintain staff, plus a recently verified second factor when that account has two-factor enabled. Redirects to /app, or to /admin/mfa when the second factor needs re-verifying.

Authorisation runs before any client is opened

This ordering is a tested contract rather than a convention. A page or action must complete its guard before it constructs a Supabase client, so an unauthorised request never reaches a query builder that holds the service-role key. The test suite asserts the ordering on the screens that read across companies.

04

Identity and tenancy

Clerk knows who you are. Postgres decides what you may see. The two are joined by one token, and the join is what makes a revoked membership take effect instantly across the whole product.

A Clerk session becomes a Supabase-trusted session through an access-token callback that asks Clerk for a token minted from a JWT template named supabase. Supabase has that Clerk instance registered as a trusted third-party issuer, so the Clerk user id arrives inside Postgres as auth.jwt() ->> 'sub'. No Supabase session is created, stored or refreshed.

How tenancy is resolved inside the database

Tenancy is not a column that application code sets. Two security-definer helpers derive it, and every row level security policy is written in terms of them.

  • current_company_id() reads the Clerk subject out of the JWT and returns the company_id of that user's accepted membership row. A membership that exists but has no accepted_at resolves to nothing, so it grants nothing.
  • current_company_employs(worker) answers whether the caller's company holds the worker's open employment row. Worker access therefore moves the moment employment moves, with no permission list to update.

Row level security is enabled on every table in the public schema, so an absent policy denies rather than permits. Tables that carry both sides of a deal, including match, engagement, worker_transfer and the crew rows beneath them, have all privileges revoked from the signed-in role outright. Companies read them only through per-role projection views.

Rate confidentiality is a view, not a query convention

supplier_match_view exposes the supplier rate. buyer_match_view exposes the all-in buyer rate. Neither exposes the platform fee, and the base table is unreachable from a tenant session. A margin cannot leak through a mis-scoped query because there is no query that could carry it.

The same view also withholds the counterparty. buyer_match_view filters out every proposal still awaiting the supplier, so a hiring business never learns a proposal existed until the supplying business has agreed to it. On the engagement, both identities stay null until commercial_confirmed_at is set, and that column is write-once.

Maintain staff

Maintain staff are Clerk users with no company membership at all. Staff status lives in backend publicMetadata and is read through the Clerk Backend API on every request, never from session claims. An explicit isAdmin key is authoritative, including when it is false, which is how a legacy grant is revoked. Nothing in the web application can escalate a customer account into staff: the grant is an audited command-line runbook run against Clerk.

A second factor is required when the staff account has two-factor enabled, and a session that has not verified it recently is redirected to /admin/mfa. Enrolment itself is optional, so an account that has never enrolled reaches the console on a single factor.

05

Registration and intake

Exactly two code paths create a company. A business can register itself, or Maintain can stand it up from a lead. Both land in the same place: a Pending company that can prepare but cannot trade.

Path one: a company registers itself

  1. A visitor creates a sign-in at /signup. Clerk owns the credential and the email verification. No company data is collected here.
  2. Clerk forces the new user to /onboarding, which collects the legal name, trading name, contact details, industry, primary region, other operating regions and, optionally, the ABN.
  3. One security-definer function creates the company, expands the operating regions with the primary region always included, binds the submitter as an already-accepted member, and writes the audit row. It refuses to run without a verified Clerk identity and email.
  4. The company lands on status Pending. The membership email recorded is the Clerk-verified session email, not the contact address typed into the form.

The ABN is optional at intake, and checked properly when supplied

Requiring an ABN at the keyboard was the single largest drop-off. It is now optional. When one is supplied it must be eleven digits and must pass the real modulus-89 checksum, and that rule is enforced twice: once in the server action and once as a database check constraint, so no write path can bypass it. An ABN is unique across companies, and a blank one can never collide.

A self-serve registration whose ABN already belongs to another company is not simply refused. It is routed into the Maintain leads queue for a human to untangle, and the message shown back never names the business already holding that ABN.

Path two: Maintain stands the company up from a lead

Enquiries arrive from the public contact form, from an admin CSV import, and from a token-authenticated webhook that an external funnel tool posts to. All three land in one lead queue that no company can see: row level security is on, there is no policy, and privileges are revoked from the signed-in role.

Lead lifecycle
StateMeaningMoves on
New Captured, nobody has called yet. A Maintain admin marks it contacted.
Contacted The only state from which the lead can be resolved. Qualified or disqualified.
Qualified A Pending company now exists and the lead is linked to it. Terminal for the queue. Nothing. The work continues on the company.
Disqualified Closed with a retained reason of at least four characters, and an audit entry. Nothing.

A qualified lead produces a company with no users at all, which the model tolerates deliberately. Maintain runs that company concierge-style from the admin console and can issue or re-issue a first administrator invitation at any point. A business that will never log in can still be onboarded and traded on its behalf, with every such write flagged as admin-entered and attributed to the acting staff member.

06

Account approval

This is the gate the whole exchange rests on. A construction workforce exchange is only safe if the counterparty is a real, insured, licensed business, so Maintain does that check once per company rather than once per deal.

The company status machine

A company carries one status from a four-value enum. The set of legal moves is small, it is enforced in the database rather than in the interface, and Closed is terminal.

The verification checklist

What Maintain reviews is generated per company by a database function rather than stored as a list. Four items are always required, one is conditional, and one more is derived from the crew the company has actually loaded.

Checklist items and when they are required
ItemRequiredNeeds a fileNote
Public liability insurance Always Yes Also one of the two document types that gate matching compliance.
Workers compensation Always Yes The second matching-compliance document type.
Payment details provided Always No An attestation flag rather than an uploaded document.
ABN verified Only when an ABN is on file No A company with no ABN is told so plainly, and it does not block activation.
Trade licence One row per mandatory company-level qualification of the trades of currently employed crew Yes The requirement appears as soon as a worker of that trade is added, and disappears when they leave.
Labour-hire licence Not required Yes, when supplied Present so it can be recorded. Licensing remains under legal review.

What counts as verified

A checklist item is satisfied only when a matching document row carries both a verifier and a verification timestamp, is inside its own date window, and, for real documents, actually has a file attached. Dates are evaluated against the Brisbane calendar date.

Two consequences are worth stating because they surprise people. An ABN verification stops counting the moment the company edits its ABN, because the verified number must still equal the current one. And verification is bound to the exact value the reviewer was shown: if the ABN moves between the screen render and the click, the write is rejected rather than applied to a value nobody checked.

How approval actually works

There are two database functions that can move a company from Pending to Active, and the difference between them is the entire design of this gate.

Approval is a staff decision, not a checklist score

The strict function refuses to activate a company whose required checklist is incomplete or expired. The Maintain approval function computes exactly the same outstanding items and then records them in the audit event instead of blocking on them, stamping the decision with approval_mode: maintain_admin and a frozen snapshot of what was still missing at the moment of the decision.

Both admin screens route a Pending-to-Active request to the second function. In practice, therefore, the checklist informs the decision and never vetoes it. The approve button is never disabled because an item is outstanding; it is disabled only while the form is submitting.

This is a deliberate accommodation of how construction paperwork actually arrives, which is by phone, email and photograph over several days. The alternative, a hard gate, produces staff who work around the system. Recording the gap keeps the decision defensible without pretending the paperwork is done.

The approval transaction

  1. The caller must be Maintain staff, and must present an expected status of literally Pending. Anything else is refused before the row is touched.
  2. The company row is locked, and the live status is re-compared against the expected one. A mismatch raises a serialisation failure telling the operator to refresh, so two staff members working the same queue cannot both write a decision.
  3. The document rows are share-locked and the outstanding required items are computed.
  4. The status is written to Active under the same conditional predicate, and an audit event is appended in the same transaction carrying the before state, the decision, the approval mode and the outstanding items.
  5. After the transaction commits, the server action emails the company that it has been verified.

Rejection

Rejection requires a reason of between four and two thousand characters, enforced in the action and again in the database. It writes an audit event and emails the company the reason verbatim, and it changes no status at all. The company remains Pending, keeps its workspace, and can correct the problem and resubmit. There is no dead end and no second registration.

Suspension and closure

Suspending or closing a company is the kill switch, and it cascades inside the same transaction. Every match still awaiting the supplier or the buyer is withdrawn, and both parties are notified of each withdrawal. Existing engagements are deliberately left alone: pulling a crew off a live site because a certificate lapsed is a worse outcome than flagging it, so committed work is surfaced for Maintain to review rather than cancelled automatically.

07

Users and membership

The user model is deliberately thin. One table binds a Clerk identity to one company, and membership alone is the authority. There is no role column and no permission set to administer.

One person, one company

company_user.user_id is the primary key and it stores the Clerk user id. That one choice carries a surprising amount of the design. A person belongs to at most one company, so there is no company switcher, current_company_id() has exactly one answer, and it is structurally impossible for an identity to carry authority across two competing businesses.

Every accepted member has identical authority

There is no owner, no manager and no read-only seat. Any accepted member can edit the company profile, upload and attach compliance documents, manage crew, invite and remove other administrators, and, subject to company status, list capacity, post requirements and decide matches. Differentiation is done at the level of the company, not the person, because compliance risk is a company-level fact.

Two guards keep that from being dangerous. A member cannot remove their own access, and a company cannot be left with zero administrators.

How an invitation binds

Inviting an administrator writes nothing to the database. It creates a Clerk invitation that carries the target company id and the invited email in backend-controlled metadata, and it expires after seventy-two hours. The membership row is created on the invitee's first authenticated request, not when the invitation is sent.

  1. A member invites an email address. The invitation is refused if that person already belongs to any company, and the cross-company case is made deliberately indistinguishable from a generic failure so the response cannot be used to probe who is on the platform.
  2. The invitee signs up or signs in. On the first request that needs a company context, the application reads the Clerk metadata, checks the invited email matches the verified session email case-insensitively, and inserts the membership row already marked accepted.
  3. The invitation metadata is then consumed, which makes it single use. If that cleanup fails it is tolerated, because the membership row is unique and already wins.
  4. A unique-violation on the insert is recovered by re-reading the existing row and refusing if it names a different company or a different invited email.

Re-issuing is the recovery path, and it is the only invitation route that checks whether the Clerk account has actually been used: it refuses a target who has already signed in. A company with zero members gets a first-administrator re-issue control in the admin console, shown only when that company is Pending or Active and has a contact email on file.

Maintain staff are not members

A Maintain admin normally holds no company_user row whatsoever. Staff authority comes from Clerk metadata alone and is checked on every request. That separation is what lets a staff member operate the verification desk and the matching desk with service-role reach while every mutation still records their Clerk user id in the audit trail.

09

Supply, demand, matching

Maintain holds both sides of the book. The software computes and constrains the candidate set, and a person decides what to propose. Neither company ever sees the other's list.

How candidates are found

There is no search index. Candidate capacity for a demand line is one indexed query over capacity lines of the same trade whose window overlaps the requirement, and the candidate unit is the worker on a capacity line. Everything after that is filtering, and the filters are the product.

  • The engagement window is the intersection of the demand window and the capacity window, and every subsequent test uses that intersection rather than the requirement's own dates.
  • Region is covered when the capacity line's location matches the requirement's work region, or when that region is listed among the line's travel regions.
  • Availability is a percentage: the days of the window covered by the worker's live capacity lines, minus days already consumed by committing engagements. Zero percent excludes. Full coverage still reads as partial if the covering line's weekly hours fall short of what the requirement asks for.
  • Credentials must be held and must clear the window. A ticket that expires inside the window is not an exclusion but a warning that only an audited override can pass. A ticket mandatory for the trade excludes even when the requirement never listed it.
  • Both companies must be Active and compliant, and a company cannot match with itself.

Candidates that fail are computed and then discarded in application code rather than filtered out in SQL, specifically so the exclusion reasons stay available to the audit trail. The reviewer sees eligible candidates and greyed candidates; excluded ones are never rendered.

The match state machine

What each side can see, and when

Disclosure by stage
Stage The supplying business sees The hiring business sees
Awaiting Supplier The requested shape, its own rate, the trade, level, window and region. Nothing. The proposal is filtered out of the buyer's projection entirely.
Awaiting Buyer Its own nominations by name, its own rate. A count, coverage, and the all-in rate. No names, no supplying business, no supplier rate.
Engagement, awaiting commercial The engagement and its own crew. The engagement. Counterparty identity is still withheld.
Engagement, confirmed The counterparty. The counterparty, and the crew ticket snapshot frozen at the start of the job.

Commercial terms are frozen at proposal

The supplier rate, the platform fee in basis points and the derived buyer rate are all written onto the match at the moment it is proposed, and copied onto the engagement at acceptance. The buyer rate is the supplier rate marked up by the fee and rounded half up. The per-hour margin is simply the difference between them.

Because those values are snapshots rather than lookups, a later change to the published rate bands or the platform fee cannot retroactively move what two businesses agreed. That is also why rate bands can be edited without a deployment: they are effective-dated configuration, and every past deal already holds its own copy.

10

Engagements and transfers

Acceptance creates an engagement and commits the crew's dates in the same transaction. From that moment the terms stop moving and a database constraint makes double-booking impossible.

The engagement lifecycle

Two invariants the database refuses to break

No double-booking. An exclusion constraint on the crew row rejects any second engagement for the same worker whose committed window overlaps an existing one in a committing status. It is not a scheduling check that can race; it is a constraint, and a conflicting insert simply fails.

The commercial marker is write-once. commercial_confirmed_at can only be set alongside a pre-authorised payment, cannot be set from any status other than awaiting commercial, and once set can never be changed. Because counterparty disclosure keys off that column, the reveal cannot be forged or undone.

What is frozen, and why

At acceptance the match snapshot is copied onto the engagement and the estimate arithmetic is fixed: expected hours, expected value, expected fee. A later edit to a rate band, the platform fee or the underlying capacity line cannot move any of it.

A second snapshot is taken per worker. The crew's tickets and licences as they stood on the day the engagement began are frozen onto the engagement row, with an explicit capture date. A site induction six months later needs to know what a person held when they came on that job, not what they hold today after changing employers. Where no trustworthy snapshot exists, the screen says so rather than showing current data in its place.

Worker transfers

Crew change employers constantly in this industry, and the usual result is duplicate records and two businesses each believing they hold the same person. The platform refuses to create the duplicate. A worker's mobile and email are unique across the whole platform, so adding someone who already exists is detected at intake and turns into a transfer request rather than a second record.

The request is deliberately uninformative. It is raised from duplicate detection, never from a search, and the response tells the requester nothing: not who currently employs the person, not which worker record matched. Only on completion does the requesting side learn the identity. A speculative request cannot be used to map a competitor's crew.

Completion runs the consequences in the same transaction. The old employment row is closed and stamped as ended by transfer, a new open row is created at the requesting company, the person comes off the old employer's open capacity lines, and every live nomination naming them is knocked out. Any proposal that falls below the minimum crew size as a result is automatically declined.

A transfer can never strand a live job. Approval is refused outright while the person sits on a committing engagement, so a move cannot leave a builder a hand short mid-window. Maintain has to resolve the engagement first.

11

The data model

The database is the rulebook, not the storage. Thirty-seven tables and twelve enums carry the state machines, the locking order, the uniqueness rules and the audit trail, and application code is mostly the thing that calls them in the right order.

Invariants the schema enforces directly

  • One open employment per worker. A partial unique index means changing employer can never produce a second live record for the same person.
  • No overlapping committed work. An exclusion constraint on worker and committed window, scoped to the committing statuses, refuses the insert.
  • One engagement per match. A unique constraint on the match reference.
  • One person, one company. The membership table's primary key is the Clerk user id.
  • A valid ABN or none. A check constraint implements the modulus-89 algorithm, and a second constraint forbids the empty string.
  • A demand line cannot be re-parented into another company's request, enforced by a composite foreign key rather than by application care.

Derived rather than stored

A great deal of what the interface shows is computed at read time and never written down, which is what keeps it from going stale. A worker's marketplace state, whether they read as available, partially available, engaged or unavailable, is a pure function of their account status, their live capacity lines and their committing engagements. The fill level of a requirement counts distinct committed workers. The commit level of a capacity line is derived the same way.

Only three line statuses are ever actually written: open, withdrawn and expired. The partially-committed and fully-committed values exist in the enum and are read as live everywhere, but no code path stores them.

Every write leaves evidence

Each privileged mutation is a security-definer function that locks its rows, re-reads the facts, performs the change and appends an audit event in the same transaction. There is no state change without an attributed audit row, which is the difference between a defensible record and an email thread when two businesses disagree about a cancellation.

The functions also carry optimistic concurrency. The caller passes the status, and sometimes the exact snapshot, that it displayed to the operator. If the live row has moved, the write is rejected with a refresh-and-retry error rather than silently overwriting somebody else's decision. Two operators working the same queue cannot both commit.

12

The daily clock

Availability, credentials and proposals all go stale on their own. One scheduled transaction a night is what keeps the board honest, and without it the exchange would quietly show work that is no longer there.

A Vercel Cron calls one route at midnight Brisbane time with a bearer secret. A deployment with no secret configured rejects every call, so an unprotected environment is closed rather than open. The route refuses to run without an exact Brisbane calendar date, and Brisbane is treated as a fixed offset because Queensland has no daylight saving.

What the run does, in order

Nightly state transitions
PassEffectConsequence
Credential ageing Recomputes every company document and worker qualification into Current, Expiring Soon or Expired against today's Brisbane date. Warnings go to the owning company and to Maintain thirty days out.
Compliance knockout An expired mandatory company document, or an expired required worker ticket with no valid replacement, knocks that person out of live nominations. A proposal that falls below the minimum crew size is automatically declined. Committed work is flagged for review, never cancelled.
Line expiry Capacity lines past their last available day and demand lines past their end date move to Expired. They stop appearing in the matching queue the next morning with nobody tidying up.
Match expiry A proposal expires seven Brisbane days after it was made, or when either underlying line's window has passed. Holds release and both lines become editable again.
Engagement activation A confirmed engagement whose start date has arrived becomes Active. This is the move a person is forbidden from making by hand.
Engagement completion An active engagement whose end date has passed becomes Completed. The final on-site day still counts as committed. The crew's committed dates release with it.
Overdue flag An engagement that has reached its start date without a commercial trigger gets one audit row per day. It shows as Overdue in the console. This is the pass that catches crew about to walk on site unpaid.
Transfer escalation A request untouched for five Brisbane business days moves to Admin Review. Both companies and Maintain are notified.

All of it happens inside a single transaction that first takes a marketplace-wide advisory lock, then locks every company and every worker in a fixed order. A partial run therefore cannot half move the market, and it cannot send email about a transition that did not commit.

Email is an outbox, not a side effect

Nothing is ever sent without a durable row. Every notification is written to the database first, then handed to the provider, so a delivery failure is visible and re-sendable and never blocks or rolls back the workflow it belonged to. Sending happens after the transaction commits, with a time budget, a per-send deadline and an idempotency key per attempt.

Delivery outcomes
OutcomeMeaningWhat happens next
Sent The provider acknowledged and returned a message id. Terminal.
Deferred Another worker holds a live lease on the row. Picked up on a later sweep.
Failed An explicit provider rejection, or a local configuration error. Never retried automatically. Only an identified Maintain admin can re-send it.
Outcome unknown A timeout, an aborted request, a server error, or a database failure after the provider had already accepted. Recorded as uncertain and never downgraded to a plain failure. A retry reuses the same idempotency key so the recipient cannot be mailed twice.

Email bodies deliberately carry no rates, no worker names and no counterparty names before the related engagement is confirmed. They state the event and deep-link into the application, so forwarding a notification cannot leak a competitor's pricing or a supplier's crew. The disclosure rule lives in one template, which is what makes it auditable.

13

Why it is built this way

Almost every structural decision in this system is an answer to a specific way that sharing crew between competing businesses goes wrong. Read as a list, the architecture is a list of those failures and the thing that makes each one impossible.

Failure, and the mechanism that prevents it
What would otherwise go wrongWhat prevents it
A competitor learns your rates, and the exchange becomes a price-discovery tool used against you. Rate asymmetry is two different database views. The base table is unreachable from a tenant session, so there is no query that could carry the other side's number.
A business uses the platform to find out who is short-staffed, or to map a competitor's crew. Counterparty identity is withheld until a write-once marker is set, and that marker cannot be set without pre-authorised payment. Transfer requests disclose neither the current employer nor which worker matched.
The same person is promised to two sites for the same week. A database exclusion constraint. Not a check that can race; a constraint that refuses the insert.
Two businesses each hold a record for the same person, and payroll and licensing become ambiguous. Platform-wide uniqueness on contact details, one open employment row per person, and a formal transfer the current employer must decide.
An employer sits on a transfer request to keep someone from leaving. Five Brisbane business days of silence escalates the decision to Maintain, and both sides are told.
A crew is pulled off a live site because a certificate lapsed overnight. Expiry knocks people out of pending proposals, but committed work is flagged for a human to review and never cancelled automatically.
An agreed price moves because somebody edited a rate band afterwards. The supplier rate, the fee and the derived buyer rate are snapshotted onto the match at proposal and copied to the engagement.
Crew arrive on a job nobody has agreed to pay for. The engagement sits visibly at Awaiting Commercial, and the nightly job raises an overdue flag the moment its start date passes.
Unverified businesses trade on the exchange. Capacity and demand intake require status Active, enforced in the server action and again under a row lock inside the transaction.
A suspended business keeps trading because the interface only hid the buttons. Company status is a row level security predicate, so a suspended company cannot write even by calling the database API directly.
Two operators working the same queue overwrite each other. Every privileged function takes the status the operator was shown and refuses the write if the row has moved.
A screen shows an empty roster because a token expired, and somebody staffs a job off it. Reads fail visibly. A failed read renders an error, never a zero, and a null metric prints the word Unavailable rather than a number.
A dispute six months later comes down to whose email is more convincing. Every state change writes an attributed audit event in its own transaction, including the frozen ticket snapshot for each worker on the job.

The concierge model is a product decision, not a stopgap

Every introduction being made by a person is what lets the platform sit between direct competitors at all. Maintain holds both sides of the book, so neither business has to publish its client list, its labour costs or its crew to the other. Automating the introduction is on the roadmap; automating it before the trust exists would be the wrong order.

The same reasoning runs through the concierge entry path. A site supervisor with a phone and no appetite for a web form can still trade: a Maintain admin transcribes the capacity, the requirement or the crew onto that company's own records, every such write is flagged as admin-entered, carries an evidence note and is attributed to the acting staff member. A relayed decision also carries an expected-version token, so a phone call that changes between the conversation and the keystroke is rejected rather than recorded as agreement.

Operating a marketplace with a small team

One deployable, one database and two managed services is a deliberate ceiling on operational surface. A build is refused outright when its environment variables are missing, malformed or mismatched, because a deployment that reports success and then serves errors on every page is the worst available failure. A separate check makes it structurally hard to ship the key that bypasses every tenant boundary into a browser bundle.

Operational notes

A few behaviours in the current build are correct but surprising, and are worth knowing before reading the code or diagnosing a support question.

  • The verification checklist never blocks approval. A strict, checklist-enforcing activation path exists in the database, but both admin screens route a Pending-to-Active request to the staff-override function instead. In production the checklist informs the decision and records what was outstanding; it does not veto it. An audit consumer can tell the two paths apart by the presence of approval_mode.
  • Pending cannot be suspended. The only exits from Pending are Active and Closed. Suspension applies to a business that was trading.
  • Rejection is not a status. It is an audit event plus an email. Anything that counts rejected accounts has to read the audit trail, not the company table.
  • There is no company-level role. Every accepted member has full authority. Anything that needs a read-only company seat needs a schema change, not a settings change.
  • Staff MFA is conditional. The second factor is required only for accounts that have enabled two-factor in Clerk. An account that has never enrolled reaches the whole admin console on one factor.
  • A blocked Active company gets the suspended wording. The requireActiveCompany guard distinguishes Pending from everything else, so a Closed company acting on a match is told it is suspended. The behaviour is correct; the copy is not precise.
  • A role change in Clerk lands on the next request. There is no Clerk webhook, which is exactly why the edge gate defers the role decision to the request itself.
  • Middleware is named proxy.ts. Searching for middleware.ts in this repository finds nothing and gives the false impression there is no edge gate.
